# Digital Fatigue & Academic Burnout — EDA Project

## 📌 Project Overview

Digital devices have become an essential part of students' academic and daily lives. However, prolonged screen exposure, academic workload, and insufficient rest can contribute to digital fatigue, stress, mental exhaustion, and academic burnout.

This project uses **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)** to examine the relationship between students' digital usage patterns, digital fatigue, academic stress, sleep, motivation, and academic burnout.

The analysis is based on questionnaire responses collected from students and focuses on identifying patterns, relationships, and trends within the collected data.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

The main objectives of this project are to:

* Understand students' digital usage patterns.
* Analyze indicators of digital fatigue such as eye strain, headaches, and difficulty focusing.
* Examine academic burnout and well-being.
* Explore the relationship between digital usage, stress, sleep, motivation, and burnout.
* Identify correlations among important academic and well-being variables.
* Derive meaningful insights from the collected questionnaire data.

---

## 📊 Dataset

The dataset was collected through a structured questionnaire covering areas such as:

* Student profile
* Mode of study
* Device usage
* Daily screen time
* Purpose of digital usage
* Online activity hours
* Digital fatigue symptoms
* Academic workload and stress
* Sleep duration and sleep quality
* Digital habits before sleep
* Academic burnout
* Mental exhaustion
* Academic motivation
* Impact of digital fatigue on academic productivity
* Strategies for reducing digital fatigue and burnout

**Number of responses:** 72
**Number of variables:** 30

---

## 🔍 Analysis Performed

### 1. Data Cleaning

The dataset was prepared for analysis by:

* Checking missing values
* Identifying duplicate records
* Standardizing categorical and Likert-scale responses
* Preparing variables for statistical analysis
* Creating derived variables required for further analysis

### 2. Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive analysis was performed to understand the overall characteristics and distribution of the collected responses.

### 3. Respondent Profile

The analysis explored the characteristics of respondents based on relevant demographic and academic variables.

### 4. Digital Usage Analysis

This section examined:

* Daily digital device usage
* Primary purpose of screen usage
* Online activity hours
* Device preferences
* Digital habits before sleep

### 5. Digital Fatigue Analysis

Digital fatigue was examined using indicators such as:

* Eye strain
* Headaches
* Impact of headaches on productivity
* Difficulty focusing after prolonged screen exposure
* Break-taking habits
* Awareness of the negative effects of prolonged screen time

### 6. Academic Burnout & Well-being

The project analyzed:

* Academic burnout levels
* Mental exhaustion
* Academic motivation
* Academic workload-related stress
* Sleep duration
* Sleep quality
* Relationship between digital fatigue and academic productivity

### 7. Relationship & Correlation Analysis

Relationships between key variables were explored using visualizations and correlation analysis.

The analysis particularly examined relationships involving:

* Overall stress
* Academic burnout
* Academic motivation
* Mental exhaustion
* Sleep quality

A correlation heatmap was also created to visualize the strength and direction of relationships among selected variables.

---

## 📈 Key Insights

The analysis provides an overview of how students' digital habits and academic well-being interact.

Key areas of insight include:

* Patterns in students' daily digital usage
* Common symptoms associated with digital fatigue
* Academic stress and burnout patterns
* The role of sleep and motivation in student well-being
* Relationships between stress, mental exhaustion, motivation, and academic burnout
* The extent to which digital fatigue is perceived to affect academic productivity

> **Note:** The findings presented in the notebook are based on the collected sample and should not automatically be generalized to all students.
