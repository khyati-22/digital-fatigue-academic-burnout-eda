# Key Findings

This file summarizes the major findings identified from the Exploratory Data Analysis of the **Digital Fatigue & Academic Burnout** dataset.

## Respondent Profile

* The dataset contains responses collected from students participating in the study.
* The respondent profile was analyzed using relevant academic and demographic characteristics.

## Digital Usage

* Students reported varying levels of daily digital device usage.
* Digital devices were used for multiple purposes, including academic activities and other daily activities.
* Online activity and screen exposure were examined to understand students' overall digital usage patterns.

## Digital Fatigue

* The analysis examined common indicators of digital fatigue, including eye strain, headaches, difficulty focusing, and the impact of prolonged screen exposure.
* Break-taking habits and awareness of the effects of prolonged screen time were also analyzed.

## Academic Burnout & Well-being

* Academic burnout was assessed using students' self-reported burnout levels and related well-being indicators.
* Academic stress, mental exhaustion, motivation, sleep duration, and sleep quality were considered as important aspects of student well-being.
* The analysis highlights the interaction between academic demands and students' overall well-being.

## Relationship Analysis

* Relationships between academic burnout and key variables were explored through comparative visualizations.
* Overall stress, academic motivation, mental exhaustion, and sleep quality were examined in relation to academic burnout.
* Correlation analysis was used to identify the direction and strength of relationships among selected variables.

## Overall Observation

The EDA provides a structured understanding of digital usage, digital fatigue, academic stress, and burnout among the surveyed students. The findings highlight the importance of examining both digital habits and academic well-being together when studying student burnout.

> **Note:** The findings are based on the collected sample and should be interpreted within the context and limitations of the study.
